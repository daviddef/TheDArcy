# Request — the will of Robert Darcy, 4th Earl of Holderness (d. 1778)

**What it is:** a Prerogative Court of Canterbury will, TNA series **PROB 11**. Searchable by name in
**Discovery** (`discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk`) and **downloadable immediately as a PDF** for a
few pounds. No letter, no waiting.

**Why it matters:** this is the one document that could *positively* support the family tradition.
The Hornby descent requires that Robert D'Arcy (b. c. 1750–51) was a **natural son** of an Earl of
Holderness. Eighteenth-century peers who acknowledged natural children commonly did so in their
wills — an annuity, a legacy, a trust. If the 4th Earl's will names a Robert, that changes everything.
**If it names no natural children at all, that is nearly as useful**, and the archive will say so.

## The search
In Discovery, search **PROB 11** for **Darcy** *or* **D'Arcy**, died **1778**.
He is **Robert Darcy, 4th Earl of Holderness**, died **16 May 1778**, of Hornby Castle, Yorkshire,
and Sion Hill. The will was probably proved mid-to-late 1778.

Watch for the spelling: contemporary clerks write **Darcy**, **D'Arcy** and **Dárcy**, and the
peerage title may be indexed as **Holderness** or **Holdernesse**.

## What to look for in it
1. Any **natural, reputed or base-born child** named or provided for — and especially any **Robert**.
2. Legacies to anyone surnamed **Darcy / D'Arcy** who is *not* one of his three legitimate children
   (George d. 1747, Thomas b. and d. 1750, Lady Amelia).
3. Annuities to unnamed persons "for reasons known to me" — the standard eighteenth-century formula
   for exactly this situation.
4. Any Royal Engineers or Ordnance connection, or a legacy to a serving officer.
