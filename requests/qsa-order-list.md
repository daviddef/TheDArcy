# Request — Queensland State Archives: nine items to order

**Updated 9 September 2026.** The whole QSA index was re-read through the site's own JSON API
(`/api/fetch?qsa_id=ITM…&type=archival_object` — no login, no captcha, curl-able), and every item
below has been identified to series, agency and file number. **None of them is digitised**, so each
needs a copy order or a reading-room visit.

QSA copy orders: <https://www.archivessearch.qld.gov.au> → find the item → "Request a copy".

## The two that would change a page

### 1. `ITM1412361` — SNEYD, Arthur Hartley
**Intestacy Files – Brisbane (`S335`) · Public Trust Office · file 687/1917 · 26 Mar 1916 – 30 Nov 1917 · Open**

The estate of the soldier killed at Pozières. Note the **start date: 26 March 1916** — five days
before he embarked on the *Star of Victoria*. That is when a soldier made his will, in his paybook,
on the way out. The file was closed 30 November 1917, fifteen months after he died.

It should show what a private soldier left, who claimed it, and — because the Public Curator had to
establish next of kin — a statement of his family. <a href="/finding-arthur">The search for him</a>

### 2. `ITM1056950` — SNEYD, William Hartley
**Insolvency Files – Public Curator Office, Brisbane (`S15200`) · file 230/1873 · 6 Oct – 3 Nov 1873 · Open**

**A wholly new fact.** He went insolvent in 1873, aged thirty-six, as a contractor. An insolvency file
lists debts, creditors, assets and the trade he was carrying on. Read beside his widow's sworn
statement in 1902 that he "had not been able to work for about 12 years", it is the other end of a
working life the tree recorded as two dates.

## Police staff files — `S1108`, Queensland Police Service

| Item | Person | File | Dates |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ITM564421` | SNEYD, Hanley | AF1623 | 7 Dec 1881 – 28 Feb 1883 |
| `ITM564398` | SNEYD, Samuel Charles | AF1599 | 8 May 1890 – 30 Sep 1897 |

**Neither is the gaol governor** — he died in 1885, before both ranges. On dates they are his sons
Samuel Hanley Hanley Stafford Sneyd (1851–1901) and Samuel Charles Sneyd the younger (1863–1938),
which would mean the family put at least two more men into the Queensland Police. **Ordering either
would confirm the identification**, which currently rests on arithmetic alone.

## One long shot, and it is free

`ITM86903` — **Photographic Records, Descriptions and Criminal Histories of Released Prisoners**
(`S14872`), Queensland Police Service, **229 digital representations, already digitised**. It surfaced
in a search for *Sneyd*. Whether that is because a Sneyd appears as an officer, a signatory or a
prisoner is not established. Free to view; worth twenty minutes.
