# Request — Queensland Births, Deaths and Marriages: death certificates

**What it is:** the Queensland historical index is free at `familyhistory.bdm.qld.gov.au`, and
certificates can be ordered online. Deaths are public after 30 years, so all three below are
available.

**Updated 9 September 2026:** the free index has now been searched for the whole SNEYD surname —
60 death registrations — so items 4–6 below carry their **exact registration numbers**, which is all
the order form needs. The D'Arcy surname has not yet been swept the same way; doing that would
supply numbers for items 1–3 too, and is the obvious next step before ordering anything.

**Why it matters:** a Queensland death certificate of this period names **the deceased's parents,
including the mother's maiden name**, the cause of death, the informant, and the length of residence
in the colony. For this family that is three separate problems solved at once.

## 1. Lindesay Atkinson D'Arcy — died 10 November 1936, Brisbane
**Why:** the *Courier-Mail* says only that he died "suddenly", aged 44. **The cause of death is
still unknown**, and it is the hinge of the whole next generation — his son Kenneth was nine, and
the family's circumstances collapsed. Index spelling may be **Lindsay** or **Lindesay**.

## 2. Florence Isabel D'Arcy — died 22 August 1918, Brisbane, aged 24
**Why:** to confirm the recorded cause, *"chlorosis; valvular disease of the heart"*, and to settle
the burial. The Brisbane City Council register has an **F J D'Arcy** at Nundah two days after her
death, and the archive has assumed the J is a mistranscription of I. The certificate would confirm
the identity and name her parents.

## 3. George Pitt D'Arcy — died 21 March 1931, Banyo, Brisbane
**Why:** he was found dead in his kitchen and there was an **inquest**, so the certificate should
carry a coronial finding. It will also settle the age discrepancy for good — the newspaper says 76,
the cemetery record says born 5 May 1864 (so 66). **And it names his parents**, which would confirm
George Lindsay D'Arcy and Eliza Keeling from a civil record for the first time.

## What to send back
Scans or photographs of all three. Every field matters, but especially **father's name, mother's
name and maiden name, informant, cause, and duration of residence in the colony**.

## Free first step, no cost at all
Before ordering, search the **free index** for each of the three and send me the index entries —
they give the registration number and often the parents' names, and may answer some of this for
nothing.

## 4. Vivian Ernest William Sneyd — died 16 February 1920, Brisbane
**Registration: `1920/B/31382`** · index gives parents **Thomas George Sneyd** and **Annie Spalding**

**Why this is now the most valuable certificate on the list.** He was a labourer, once rejected for
being too short, who enlisted in December 1915, was wounded with the 31st Battalion at the end of
September 1917, was admitted with **albuminuria** at Birmingham in May 1918, spent eight months in
English hospitals, and came home on a hospital ship on 5 January 1919. He died **thirteen months
later**, aged 29.

The certificate names the cause. If it is nephritis or its consequences, he is a war death that the
official count never made, and the archive can say so. Until then `/great-war` says only that he
died and declines to name a cause. <https://www.awm.gov.au> does not commemorate him.

## 5. William Hartley Sneyd — died 11 September 1902, Brisbane
**Registration: `1902/B/2776`** · index gives **date of birth 30/09/1837**, mother **Catherine**,
father **Samuel Sneyd**

**Why:** the coronial file is already read and published in full, so the cause is known. What the
certificate adds is the **informant** — who reported the death, and what they said about his birth
and his years in the colony. The index's 30/09/1837 has already corrected an error this archive
published; the certificate is the underlying record.

## 6. Martha Sneyd, née Blum — died 19 December 1904
**Registration: `1904/C/1454`** · index gives parents **John Blum** and **Mary Ann O'Brien**

**Why:** John Blum is one of the archive's thinnest people — "born Germany 1842", no town, no ship.
His wife's name is new as of today. The certificate would give Martha's birthplace and, with luck,
her father's occupation and origin, which is the way into the Blum question.
