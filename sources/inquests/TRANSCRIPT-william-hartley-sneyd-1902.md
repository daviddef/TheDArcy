## 1. Police summary (typed)

> re William Hartley Sneyd – deceased.
>
> On the 12th September deceased was found lying dead in Victoria Park Brisbane with a bottle beside
> him containing some liquid which was afterwards on analysis found to contain crude carbolic acid.
> Deceased had been suffering from asthma for about 12 years and he often stated he wished some one
> would put him out of his misery. A Postmortem was made by Dr Dods and the stomach and other organs
> were forwarded to the Government Analyst who certified that he found crude carbolic in the stomach
> and intestines.
>
> The case was apparently one of suicide.
> No suspicious circumstances.

## 3. Deposition of JOHN WILLIAMS, labourer, of Roche Street, Spring Hill

> I remember 12th September inst about 10 A.M. I was in company of Alfred Hutchison walking along the
> railway line in Victoria Park. I saw a man lying between two stacks of rails. I went up to within
> three feet of him & said to Hutchison "That man dead". I went & reported the matter to
> **Mrs. Costello, caretaker of the park**. I remained with the body until the arrival of Const.
> Moroney when I saw remove the body. I visited the spot later in the day & found near where the body
> had been a bottle containing some liquid. (The bottle produced is the same) which I handed to the
> police.

## 4. Deposition of MIRIAM SNEYD, his widow

> I am the widow of the late William Hartley Sneyd. I remember the morning of 11th September instant,
> my husband left home a little before 9 o'clock. I heard next day about 11 o'clock by the police that
> his body had been found in Victoria Park. His body was brought here by the Undertaker the same
> evening.
>
> I did not notice anything unusual in his appearance when he last left home. But his health was not
> so good as usual. He had been suffering from **Asthma for 10 or 12 years** & had a very bad attack
> about two months ago. **He has never threatened to take his life but when suffering greatly has said
> he wished some one would take him out of his misery.**
>
> I was not aware he had purchased any carbolic or other poison. I never saw the bottle produced
> before.
>
> **He had not been able to work for about 12 years in consequence of an injury to his arm.**
>
> His age was **64 years**, born in […], **his father's name was Samuel Sneyd, Gaol Governor, his
> mother's name was Catherine**. — Hislop was the Undertaker, he was a Contractor, he was married at
> Brisbane when 22 years, to Miriam Wakefield. **Thomas Ge[orge] 41, Louisa Miriam 39, Catherine
> Isabel 37, Arthur William Hartley 35, Ernest Ephraim 33, Ada Zillah 30 — and the whole living.**
>
> — *Miriam Sneyd*

## 5. Deposition of EDWARD LAW, retired, of Hartley Street off Gregory Terrace

> I know the deceased William Hartley Sneyd for many years. I remember the 11th Sept instant I saw
> Sneyd about 4 [o'clock] on that day walking along Gregory Terrace. **I remarked to him that he was
> looking well & he passed on.** I had not seen him since.
>
> The following morning I heard his body had been found in Victoria Park.
>
> — *Edward Law × his mark*  (he could not write)

## 6. Deposition of PATRICK MORONEY, police constable, Brisbane

> I remember about 10.20 A.M. on 12th instant in consequence of something I heard I went to a spot in
> Victoria Park beside the Railway line & **about 200 yards from Gregory Terrace**. I there found the
> body of William Hartley Sneyd lying between 2 stacks of railway rails. John Williams was there.
>
> I examined the body & **found no mark of violence**. In the clothing I found a knife, a pair of
> spectacles, & 2 handkerchiefs, **which I handed to a son of deceased**.
>
> I had the body removed to the morgue. On the same day I was present when a post mortem was made by
> Dr Dods. The doctor handed me the certificate I now produce (marked "A"). The doctor also handed me
> a sealed jar containing the stomach & other organs of deceased. The same evening Const. Henry handed
> me the bottle produced. On the morning of 13th I handed these to the Government Analyst. I produce a
> certificate from the Govt Analyst (marked "B").
>
> — *P. Moroney*

## 7. Post-mortem certificate

> **Hospital Morgue, Brisbane, Sept 12 1902.** I hereby certify that on the 12 day of September 1902,
> I made a Post-mortem Examination of the body of a male named **Wm Hartley Sneyd**, by order of
> **E. M. Murray, Esq., P.M.**, and I forwarded in a sealed jar, Stomach with contents, a portion of
> the Liver, Kidney, and Spleen to the Government Analyst.
>
> — Government Medical Officer [Dr Dods]. *Marginal note: "Burial order given 12/9/02."*

## 8. Certificate of the Government Analyst

> I, the undersigned, Government Analyst for the State of Queensland, do hereby certify that I
> received on the **13th day of September 1902**, from Constable Moroney, jar containing Stomach and
> other organs "W. H. Sneyd", also one bottle containing dark liquid, and have Analysed the same, and
> declare the result to be as follows:—
>
> **I found 1¼ ounces of crude carbolic acid in the stomach.** The part of the intestines submitted
> also contained crude carbolic acid. The bottle contained crude carbolic acid. The crude carbolic
> acid consisted of 30 per cent of Carbolic and Cresylic Acids mixed with tar oils.
>
> "Taff's" Medical Jurisprudence states that the minimum fatal dose of Carbolic Acid is about ⅛ of an
> ounce.
>
> — **J. Brownlie Henderson**, Government Chemical Laboratory, Brisbane, 22 September 1902.

*(1¼ ounces against a minimum fatal dose of ⅛ of an ounce — ten times over.)*

## What this establishes for the archive

1. **The parentage, sworn.** Miriam's deposition — *"his father's name was Samuel Sneyd, Gaol
   Governor, his mother's name was Catherine"* — independently confirms the joint this archive spent
   a whole pass testing, and confirms the gaol governorship from the family's own mouth.
2. **Born 1838**, not 1837: age 64 in September 1902. Against the Find a Grave date of 30 Sept 1837.
3. **Married at Brisbane aged 22** to Miriam Wakefield — consistent with 15 November 1859.
4. **Six children living in 1902**, with ages that match the tree exactly.
5. **He was a contractor who had not worked for about twelve years, after an injury to his arm** —
   and had had asthma for ten or twelve years. He was 52 when he stopped working.
6. The undertaker was **Hislop** — the same firm, John Hislop & Sons of Peel Street, that would bury
   his grandson-in-law Lindesay Atkinson D'Arcy thirty-four years later.

## Publication

It concerns a suicide, and while the man has been dead 124 years, his descendants are living and the
archive's repository is public — so the decision was put to the family rather than taken here. The
answer, on 9 September 2026, was to publish it plainly. The page is `/inquest-1902`; `/sneyd`,
`/sources` and `/open-questions` were corrected at the same time, the last of them having said that
this file "has not been read yet".
