## 1. Lindesay Atkinson D'Arcy (1892–1936) — resolved

### 1.1 The obituary — cause and date confirmed, and the eulogy corroborated

**The Courier-Mail (Brisbane), Wednesday 11 November 1936, p. 23** — `nla.news-article37013266`

> **OBITUARY**
>
> Mr. L. A. D'Arcy.— The death occurred suddenly yesterday of Mr. Lindsay Atkinson D'Arcy, of
> Botany Street, Clayfield, who for many years had been on the staff of the Brisbane office of the
> Union Trustee Company of Australia, Ltd. Mr. D'Arcy, who was aged 44 years, was the only son of
> the late Mr. G. T. D'Arcy, of the Railway Department, and received his education at the old
> Normal School. He served with the Australian Forces in France during the Great War. The late
> Mr. D'Arcy is survived by his widow and five children. The funeral will leave the funeral chapel
> of John Hislop and Sons, Peel Street, South Brisbane, at 3.30 p.m. to-day, for the Toowong
> Cemetery.

**Funeral notice**, same paper and date, p. 1 — `nla.news-article37013394` — repeats the Botany
Street address and the 3.30 departure for Toowong.

**What this settles.** Five separate details in Ken D'Arcy's 2010 eulogy are confirmed by a
newspaper printed seventy-four years earlier, and none of them contradicted:

| The eulogy says | The obituary says |
|---|---|
| "an old Queenslander home at **Clayfield**" | "of **Botany Street, Clayfield**" |
| "Dad started work as a 15 year old at **Union Trustees where his father had worked**" | "for many years had been on the staff of the Brisbane office of the **Union Trustee Company of Australia, Ltd.**" |
| his father died "**unexpectedly**" when Ken was 9 | "The death occurred **suddenly** yesterday" |
| — | "served with the **Australian Forces in France** during the Great War" |
| five children | "survived by his widow and **five children**" |

Two small corrections to the tree: he is "**Lindsay**" in both notices, not *Lindesay*; and he is
called the **only son** of his father, which is right — George Pitt and Ida had four children and
one was a boy. The initials "G. T. D'Arcy" are wrong for **G**eorge **P**itt; either the paper or
the OCR has it.

### 1.2 The insolvency of 1926 — and what the trustee actually alleged

**Daily Standard (Brisbane), Tuesday 20 September 1927, p. 5** — `nla.news-article184914345`,
headed *Insolvent's Affairs. TRUSTEE ALLEGES FRAUD.*

The facts as reported:

- For about two and a half years to **25 July 1926** he carried on business as a **grocer at
  Cavendish Road, South Brisbane**, "with the assistance of his wife".
- About three months before that date he **left his wife to carry on the business and took a
  position as clerk in the employ of the Union Trustees**. (This is the beginning of the career the
  obituary describes.)
- On 25 July 1926 he could not continue — "not having sufficient funds, the pressure of creditors,
  heavy overhead charges, and smallness of profits" — called his creditors together and assigned
  the business to them.
- An examination disclosed a **deficiency of £1,381**. He was declared insolvent on
  **1 October 1926**; **George Rees** was appointed trustee on 3 November 1926.
- In September 1927 the trustee sued the **Bank of New South Wales, South Brisbane branch**, for
  £187 14s 1d out of £215 10s 6d that D'Arcy had paid in between 12 and 26 July 1926, alleging
  those payments were "**fraudulent and void**" as against him.

> **Read the headline carefully.** The alleged "fraud" is a claim of **fraudulent preference** —
> that a failing debtor paid down one creditor (his bank) ahead of the others in the fortnight
> before he collapsed. The action was brought **against the bank**, not against D'Arcy, and it is a
> technical avoidance claim under insolvency law, not an allegation that he stole anything. D'Arcy
> gave evidence that he had "treated the bank as an ordinary creditor, and had not given it
> preference over any other". The bank's manager gave evidence of a routine £150 monthly overdraft
> of the kind "customary to allow … to men in a small way of business".

**The date that matters.** **Kenneth Lindsay D'Arcy was born at Sandgate on 17 August 1927.** His
father is described in these reports, one month later, as "**Lindesay Atkinson D'Arcy, of
Sandgate**", standing in the Supreme Court in the wreckage of his grocery. The eulogy's account of
a family "in a spot of bother" after 1936 has a ten-year prehistory that Ken, born into the middle
of it, plainly never knew.

### 1.3 1929 — he is the witness, not the accused

Two 1929 headlines look damning in a search-results list and are the opposite on inspection.

**Daily Standard, Tuesday 6 August 1929, p. 1** — `nla.news-article187390932`, *"ROBBED PETER TO PAY
PAUL." AGENT'S ALLEGED REPLY TO DETECTIVE.* and **The Brisbane Courier, Wednesday 7 August 1929,
p. 20** — `nla.news-article21435307`, *AGENT CHARGED. ALLEGED THEFT AS SERVANT.*

The accused is **William John Rode**, 48, agent, of Clayfield — charged with stealing as a servant
of the Beak Pastoral Company, part of a deficiency of about £1,450.

**Lindsay Atkinson D'Arcy appears as a witness for the prosecution.** He was the Union Trustee
Company's agent who took over the Beak Pastoral Company's affairs from Rode; he called at Rode's
office and home, and when he said whom he represented Rode answered *"Don't say any more. I don't
wish my wife to know anything about the business."* When the promised settlement cheque did not
come, **D'Arcy contacted the C.I.D.**

So: three years after his own insolvency, he is the man who detected an embezzlement and reported
it to the police. **Do not let the search snippet stand as the story.**

### 1.4 Minor

**The Telegraph / The Brisbane Courier, 25–26 September 1929** — `nla.news-article182060518`,
`nla.news-article21488030`. "Lindsay Atkinson D'Arcy, of Hall Street, Northgate, was fined £1 10s,
with 3s 6d costs, for having used an **unlicensed wireless set**." An address, and a date, and a
very 1929 offence.

## 2. George Pitt D'Arcy (1864–1931) — how he died

**Daily Standard (Brisbane), Friday 24 April 1931, p. 8** — `nla.news-article180986554`

> At the inquiry into the death of **George Pitt D'Arcy, 76, railway pensioner**, who was found dead
> in the kitchen of his residence, in **Victoria street, Banyo**, on **March 21**, **Lindsay
> Atkinson D'Arcy, a son of deceased**, said his father had been **suffering from heart trouble,
> but had never been treated by a doctor**.

New: the place (Victoria Street, Banyo), the manner (found dead at home, alone, in the kitchen),
the inquest, and that his son gave the evidence. It also confirms "Railway Department" from the
obituary of five years later — here as "railway pensioner".

**A discrepancy to record.** The report gives his age as **76**. Born 5 May 1864, he was **66** in
March 1931. Either the reporter or the OCR has 76 for 66, or the birth year is wrong. The tree's
1864 is supported by his marriage in 1888 and his eldest child in 1890; a man born 1855 marrying at
33 is also possible but less likely. **Flagged, not resolved.**

## 3. Frederick Robert D'Arcy (1811–1875) — insolvent in 1852

108 Trove hits. Among them, an insolvency running through the second half of 1852:

- **New South Wales Government Gazette, Friday 30 July 1852, p. 1176** — `nla.news-article…` (Private
  Notices): "WHEREAS the estate of **Frederick Robert D'Arcy** was on the twentieth day of July,
  1852, placed … *In the Insolvent Estate of Frederick Robert D'Arcy, of **Hartley**, in the Colony
  of New South Wales, gentleman*."
- Special and adjourned meetings of creditors reported in the *Sydney Morning Herald* and *Empire*
  through August–October 1852, before S. F. Milford, Chief Commissioner of Insolvent Estates.

This corroborates his modern biographer's account of a man who "lurched from one disaster to
another". **Hartley** is in the Blue Mountains, which fits the surveying career.

> **A pattern, or a coincidence?** Two D'Arcys through the Insolvent Court, seventy-four years
> apart — Frederick Robert in Sydney in 1852, Lindesay Atkinson in Brisbane in 1926. It is worth
> noting and worth nothing more than that: colonial insolvency was extremely common, and two cases
> in four generations is not a trait. Recorded here so the next person does not have to rediscover
> either.

## 4. FindMyPast — Robert D'Arcy (1751–1827)

### 4.1 The burial, and where "1751" actually comes from

**Kent Burials — St Mary, Chatham** (transcript, FindMyPast; source *St Mary's Chatham 1813–1837
burials*):

| | |
|---|---|
| Name | Robert D'Arcy |
| Burial date | **19 May 1827** |
| Place | Chatham, St Mary, Kent |
| Residence | Chatham |
| **Age at death** | **76** |
| Birth year | 1751 *(derived by the indexer from the age)* |

Also indexed in *England Deaths & Burials 1538–1991* at Chatham, Kent.

**This is important for the Hornby question.** The birth year of 1751 that every version of this
pedigree repeats is **not from a baptism**. It is back-calculated from an age stated on a burial
register in 1827 — which puts his birth between about May 1750 and May 1751 and is only as good as
whoever reported the age. Nothing here names a father.

### 4.2 A null result, recorded so it is not repeated

Searched FindMyPast for **baptisms of a Robert D'Arcy / Darcy (name variants on) born c. 1741–1761**,
England, all record sets. **No baptism found** — in Yorkshire or anywhere else. The 838-result
sweep for *Robert Darcy* returns burials, marriages, wills and Irish abstracts, and not one English
baptism in the window.

**A null is a result.** It does not disprove the Yorkshire descent; it does mean the descent cannot
presently be reached from below, and that the National Archives' Royal Engineers commission papers
are now the better target.

### 4.3 Confirmed in passing

- **Hampshire Marriages, Portsea St Mary, 1779** — a Robert Darcy marriage, consistent with the
  tree's "married Jean Ward at Portsea, 21 June 1779". *Transcript not yet opened; FindMyPast's
  result rows would not open in this session.* **To do.**
- **England, Boyd's Marriage Indexes, 1779, London** — "D'Arcy, Robert Hon Amelia Baroness Conyers".
  This is the **4th Earl of Holderness's daughter Amelia**, Baroness Conyers, marrying in 1779 —
  the marriage that carried the Conyers barony out of the Darcy family. Relevant to
  [the Hornby claim](../site/src/pages/hornby.astro): it is the documentary trace of the legitimate
  line ending in a daughter, which is why an illegitimate son has to be invoked at all.
