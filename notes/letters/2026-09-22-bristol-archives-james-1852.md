Dear Holly,

Thank you — this was more useful than a positive answer would have been.

The point about the format settles something for us. We had three open lines of enquiry
resting on the assumption that the 1828 entry would name Thomas's father, and your sentence
that records of that date carry no fathers, abodes, ages, conditions or occupations retires
all three at once. We have taken that down publicly as our own error rather than yours: the
belief came from a marriage of 1871 in the same family, which does name a father, and we
carried a feature of the post-1837 registers back forty-three years without checking.

Two small things, if they are quick.

**The spelling.** We publish this marriage from the FamilySearch index, which transcribes the
surname as SANIGRE. You read the register itself and give SANIGAR. We have corrected the page
to say that the two disagree and that we have not seen the image, and we are treating your
reading as the one taken from the document. If the register hand is ambiguous at that word we
would rather say so than pick a side — is it a clear SANIGAR?

**The 1852 marriage of James.** You mention a marriage certificate from 1852 for what looks
like Thomas's son James, with Thomas's occupation given as Labourer. We do not hold that
record. James appears in this family as an eleven-year-old in the 1841 census and again in
1866 as the father of Lydia Sinegar, and there is nothing between. Could you give us the
reference for it — parish, date and register reference — so that we can order or find it
ourselves? We are not asking for a further lookup, only for where it is.

The Labourer matches the 1851 census, which is what makes it look like the right man.

With thanks for the care taken over this,

David Defranceski
