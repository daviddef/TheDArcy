**To:** Helen Crawford Walsh, RCB Library · **Re:** St Mary's Cathedral, Limerick — and the Wicklow enquiry of 16 September
**Drafted:** 21 September 2026 · **NOT SENT** — David to review, amend, decide the money, and send

> **Why this letter is shaped the way it is.** Ms Crawford Walsh answered the Wicklow enquiry on
> 16 September and offered to search the Library's registers on request, with a fee quoted on
> application. This is the reply to that offer. It does two things in one email: it names the three

Dear Ms Crawford Walsh,

Thank you for your reply of 16 September, and for the offer to search the Library's registers. I
should like to take it up, and to add a second line of the same family while I am asking.

I am in Australia and cannot visit. I am building a free, non-commercial family archive that
publishes its sources and grades every claim, so accuracy matters to me more than volume, and I
would much rather have three exact answers than thirty approximate ones. I am happy to pay the
Library's fees; I should be grateful for **a quote for each part below separately**, so that I can
decide what to commission rather than committing to the whole.

## Part one — County Wicklow, the marriage the earlier letter was about

The marriage I am looking for is **GEORGE PITT D'ARCY to MARIA WHITE**, in County Wicklow, about
1819. He was a major in the British Army; she is otherwise unidentified. No date or parish has ever
been recorded for it in anything I can reach — the marriage is asserted by family tradition and by
inherited trees, and nothing behind it has ever been seen.

In my first letter I could not name a parish, which I knew was not a reasonable thing to ask. I can
now. From your own published holdings list, the Wicklow parishes whose **marriage registers cover
1819** and which seem to me the most likely are, in order:

1. **Bray** (marriages 1670–1956)
2. **Wicklow town** (1729–1998)
3. **Arklow** (1798–1861)

**My ask:** a search of those three registers for a D'Arcy marriage between **1815 and 1823**,
allowing for the surname appearing as DARCY or D'ARCY. If you would rather quote for one parish at
a time, Bray is the one I would start with.

I should also be grateful to know whether **Betham's abstracts of the Prerogative marriage licence
bonds** are consultable at the Library, and whether they are indexed by surname. If they are, a D'Arcy
licence for that period might be quicker and cheaper than any register search.

## Part two — St Mary's Cathedral, Limerick

This is the older question and the one I would rather spend money on if I have to choose.

The one Irish record this archive has actually read is a baptism: **PAUL COLE ATKINSON, christened
at St Mary's Cathedral, Limerick, on 9 July 1832, son of RICHARD ATKINSON and ANNE.** It is indexed
in *Ireland, Births and Baptisms 1620–1881*, and the index is all I have — it gives the two parents'
names and stops.

Above that baptism my records carry five further generations in Limerick, and **not one of them rests
on a document**. They come from other people's trees. I would rather cut them than keep publishing
them unevidenced, and the register entry itself is the shortest way to find out which they are.

**My asks, in the order they would help:**

**a) The baptism entry itself, 9 July 1832.** An index entry drops everything a register keeps. I
should like a **transcript or a copy of the original entry**, particularly the father's **abode and
occupation** and any note of the mother's maiden surname.

**b) The marriage of Richard Atkinson and Anne, roughly 1826 to 1831**, at St Mary's or in another
Limerick city parish you hold. My records give her as **ANN KENT**, born about 1805, and that is
precisely the sort of unevidenced assertion I am trying to test. A marriage entry of that date would
usually name both fathers, which would settle two generations at once.

**c) Whether St Mary's baptisms survive far enough back to hold the parents' own generation.** The
names I am testing, all said to be of Limerick, are:

| Person | Said to be | Testing |
|---|---|---|
| Richard Atkinson | 1801–1865 | father of Paul Cole Atkinson |
| Ann Kent | 1805–1880 | his wife |
| Richard Atkinson | 1765–1826 | his father |
| Rose Cole | 1771–1836 | his mother — Paul Cole Atkinson carries her surname as a middle name |
| William Kent | b. 1767 | Ann's father |
| Mary Bowen | 1770–1832 | Ann's mother |

I am **not** asking you to search for all six. I am asking whether the surviving registers could
hold them at all, so I know whether this is a line worth pursuing or one to close.

**d) One more Limerick family, if the answer to (c) is encouraging: CREECH.** Paul Cole Atkinson's
wife was **JANE CREECH**, of Limerick, born about 1830. A baptism for her in the same period would
be the other half of that household.

## Part three — the coverage question, which costs nothing

Whatever is decided about fees, the single most useful thing you could tell me is simply **which
Limerick city parish registers the Library holds, and for what years**. I understand that much of
County Limerick's Church of Ireland material was lost in 1922 and that St Mary's is one of the
survivors. Knowing the actual span of what survives would let me record honestly, in public, what
can and cannot be asked of these records — which is worth as much to me as an answer about my own
family, and rather more than a search that turns up nothing because the register no longer exists.

Thank you for your time, and again for the offer that prompted this letter.

With best wishes,

## Notes for David before sending

- **This is two enquiries in one email on purpose.** She is the person for both, the Wicklow one is
  already open with her, and a second cold email about Limerick would be starting again.
- **The money.** Nothing here commits to a fee. Every ask is phrased as "please quote", and Part
  three is free. If only one part is to be paid for, **Part two (a) — the 1832 register entry —
  is the best value**: it is a single named entry on a known date, so it should be the cheapest
  thing on the list, and it is the only Irish record this archive has ever read.
- **Part two (c) is the honest one.** Five Limerick generations are published here as family lore,
  chipped as unevidenced, and if the registers cannot reach them they should be cut back rather
  than left standing. The letter says so.
- **Why not RootsIreland instead.** It would need an account and a subscription in someone's name,
  and this archive does not create accounts. The RCB route is the same records at their source.
- **If she quotes a fee you do not want to pay**, Part three still leaves the archive better off
  than it is now — it converts "Limerick is a hard county" from something read on a website into a
  stated holding with years attached.
