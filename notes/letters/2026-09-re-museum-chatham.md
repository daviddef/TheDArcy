**Re: Major-General Robert D'Arcy, C.B. (d. Chatham 1827) and 2nd Lt Constantine D'Arcy (d. Chatham 1805)**

**Send to:** the Enquiries Service, Royal Engineers Museum, Library & Archive, Prince Arthur Road,

Dear Archivist,

I am writing on behalf of a small, non-commercial family archive, and I would like to commission
your Enquiries Service for the paid research described below. I am happy to pay the £25 fee and any
reproduction costs, and I am in no hurry.

My subject is **Major-General Robert D'Arcy, C.B., Royal Engineers**. I have established the
following from Connolly and Edwards, *Roll of Officers of the Corps of Royal Engineers from 1660 to
1898* (R.E. Institute, Chatham, 1898), where he is **entry 203, page 10**:

| | |
|---|---|
| Second Lieutenant | 17 January 1776 (no prior ensigncy) |
| Lieutenant | 1 October 1784 |
| Captain-Lieutenant | 15 August 1793 |
| Captain | 3 March 1797 |
| Lieutenant-Colonel | 29 April 1802 |
| Colonel | 4 June 1813 |
| Major-General | 12 August 1819 |
| War services | Minorca 1781; Portugal 1798; Minorca 1798; Copenhagen 1807; Walcheren 1809 |
| Remarks | "Died at Chatham, 13 5 1827" |

I can add, from records outside the Corps: he married **Jean (Jane) Ward at Portsea St Mary on
21 June 1779**; a son **Constantine** was christened at **St Michael, Barbados, on 18 February 1786**,
the entry naming both parents, which suggests a garrison posting to the West Indies in the later
1780s; and he was **buried at St Mary, Chatham, on 19 May 1827, aged 76**. He is described in the
family as having commanded the Royal Engineers at Chatham.

**My question is a single one, and Connolly cannot answer it: who were his parents?**

The family tradition — which I am treating as unproven and am equally happy to see disproved — is
that he was a natural son of one of the Darcy Earls of Holderness. No baptism for him has been found
in any indexed English collection, there is no notice of his death in the *Gentleman's Magazine* for
May–July 1827, and I understand that WO 149 (the Woolwich cadet registers, now at Sandhurst) begins
too late to include a cadet of the early 1770s.

I should be grateful for your advice, and for a search where you think one is warranted, on:

1. Whether the Corps' own holdings — officers' services, Ordnance or Academy papers, biographical
   files, or the Institution's manuscript collections — contain **anything giving his parentage,
   birthplace, age on commission, or the name of a sponsor or patron** for his commission of
   17 January 1776.
2. What survives, and where, to document the **admission of Gentlemen Cadets at Woolwich in the
   early 1770s** — given that WO 149 starts later. If the Board of Ordnance minutes or warrant books
   are the answer, I would welcome a pointer to the right series.
3. Whether you hold **personal papers, correspondence, portraits or a memorial** for him, or any note
   of his tenure as Commanding Royal Engineer at Chatham.
4. Anything at all on his son **Second Lieutenant Constantine D'Arcy**, Royal Engineers — commissioned
   1 February 1804, lieutenant 1 March 1805, and recorded by Connolly as having "Died at Chatham,
   6 5 1805", aged about nineteen. A cause, a burial, or a note of the circumstances would be
   welcome.

If the honest answer to any of these is that nothing survives, that is genuinely useful to me and I
would be glad to be told so plainly — the archive records negative results as carefully as positive
ones.
