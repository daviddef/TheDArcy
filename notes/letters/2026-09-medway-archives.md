# To: Medway Archives and Local Studies Centre
**Re: St Mary's, Chatham — two D'Arcy burials, 1805 and 1827**

Subject line: *Copies of two burial entries, St Mary Chatham: D'Arcy 1805 and 1827*

**Send to:** Medway Archives and Local Studies Centre, Bryant Road, Strood, Rochester, Kent ME2 3EP.
**Note:** this is the cheapest and most likely-to-succeed of all the enquiries. It asks for two
specific entries, both already located by index, and both should exist.

---

Dear Archivist,

I am compiling a small non-commercial family archive and would like to order copies of two entries
in the burial register of **St Mary's, Chatham**, together with any accompanying detail the register
carries.

**1. Robert D'Arcy, buried 19 May 1827.**
Indexed in *Kent Burials* from the register *"St Mary's Chatham 1813–1837 burials"*, giving:
residence **Chatham**, **age at death 76**. He was **Major-General Robert D'Arcy, C.B., Royal
Engineers**, who died on 13 May 1827. I should like a copy or image of the register entry itself, in
case it records anything the index omits — rank, abode, or the officiating minister.

**2. Constantine D'Arcy, died 6 May 1805.**
This one I have *not* found in an index, and it is the more interesting request. Connolly and
Edwards, *Roll of Officers of the Corps of Royal Engineers*, entry 335, records Second Lieutenant
Constantine D'Arcy — commissioned 1 February 1804, lieutenant 1 March 1805 — with the remark
**"Died at Chatham, 6 5 1805"**. He was about nineteen, and he was Robert D'Arcy's son: his christening
at St Michael, Barbados, on 18 February 1786 names his father Robert and mother Jane.

I should be grateful if you could check the **St Mary's Chatham burial register for May–June 1805**
for his entry, and supply a copy. If he is not there, the **garrison chapel** or another Chatham
parish may hold him, and I would welcome your advice on which registers to try. Please note the
surname is written variously **D'Arcy, Darcy and D'Arey** in contemporary sources.

I am happy to pay your standard search and reproduction fees, and will cite Medway Archives in full.
The archive is public, free and carries no advertising: **https://daviddef.github.io/TheDArcy/**
